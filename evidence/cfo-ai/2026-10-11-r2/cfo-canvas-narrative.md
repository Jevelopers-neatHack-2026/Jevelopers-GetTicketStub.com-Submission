## Decision: validate a $9 digital keepsake before scaling

TicketStub, built by Jevelopers / neatHack, is a **pre-revenue hackathon prototype**. It turns an event link and personal memories into a researched, editable commemorative ticket design. It is a digital keepsake, **not an admission ticket**. The initial offer is one digital design for $9, with no subscription, one design/charge per order and no basket savings.

**All financial projections on this page are assumptions, not actual sales. Forecast amounts are USD; the actual-expenses table preserves original source currency.** Total measured project costs and actual revenue remain unreconciled: no complete reconciled invoices, usage logs or paid order receipts were supplied. Two identified expenses are recorded separately as owner/provider-reported, not settled or independently reconciled. Sandbox checkout is not revenue. No validated demand, market size, customer traction, signed organizer partners or production readiness is claimed. The $1,000 opening balance is hypothetical—not the team’s actual cash.

The Base hypothesis is 10,000 monthly visits, 10% studio starts and 2% paid conversion of visits: 1,000 starts and 200 digital orders. It projects $1,800 revenue and $702.81 monthly operating profit before founder pay, versus a $2,297.19 loss with $3,000 monthly founder pay. This is a willingness-to-pay and usage-control hypothesis, not a sustainable business proven by the demo.

Bootstrap’s unpaid founder labor is a real omission from economic sustainability, not free labor. Three months of illustrative prelaunch spending plus six months of postlaunch protection requires **$2,416.011 bootstrap or $25,199.151 with founder pay**, before unpriced tax/legal/insurance obligations. The $1,000 opening assumption does not finance that ramp. Do not confuse a cash-positive postlaunch month with a funded launch.

## Product, customers and differentiation

Target customers are concert, sports and theatre attendees plus gift buyers. The intended job is to preserve the story of an event in a personalized collectible rather than another screenshot. The prototype’s product scope includes research and fact review, ticket styles, art remix, personal details, sharing/wall, matching sets, gifts and organizer flows. Personalization and event provenance are **differentiation hypotheses**; facts and intellectual-property rights still need review.

Alternatives include generic design tools, screenshots and commodity printers. Generic tools offer flexibility but leave event research and assembly to the user; screenshots are free but less personal; printers provide output without necessarily supplying trustworthy event context. TicketStub must prove that convenience and personal significance merit $9. Do not claim a market-size figure or defensible moat without evidence.

Launch in one community/event with digital-only delivery. Run referral and sharing tests, invite small opt-in cohorts, observe willingness-to-pay and record conversion of visits—not conversion of studio starts. Organizers are a future referral/distribution channel, not signed partners or forecast revenue. Matching sets and gift flows are future demand tests; no uplift is assumed. Physical products, shipping income, foil and holographic upsells contribute **zero forecast sales in every scenario**.

## Scenario mechanics and how to edit

Main Scenario is the **Base** case. TicketStub — Conservative and TicketStub — Growth are separate editable futures. The scenario comparison reads those saved futures, not additive case totals. For Main, edit the tested table 01 driver cells **01!B5, B6, B7, B10, B32 and B34** in the native XLSX (full worksheet name: “01 — Editable assumptions — USD”). Table 04 reads those Main inputs; B33 is calculated. The saved app uses the corresponding named assumption rows, with opening cash entered on its “Opening cash” child. Scenario alternatives retain their own traffic/conversion/usage/budget inputs. Input-to-output tests cover these six Main drivers; do not assume that editing a linked result is an input change. At zero orders, per-order AI, contribution, margin and break-even are n/a; aggregate contribution, operating profit, burn and funding stay numeric. Non-positive contribution also makes break-even n/a. The prefix TS keeps TicketStub variables separate from future business models.

Conservative/Base/Growth assume visits 5,000/10,000/25,000; studio start rate 10% each; paid conversion of visits 1%/2%/4%; chats/start 8/4/3; total images/start 4/2/1.5 (one initial image plus expected revisions 3/1/0.5). Fractional images are averages, not a fractional image delivered to a person. Steady-state operating profits before founder pay are -$161.1174/$702.81/$5,070.36875; with $3,000 founder pay they are -$3,161.1174/-$2,297.19/$2,070.36875.

AI/start = (research cost + chats × chat cost + total images × image cost) × (1 + funding allowance) × (1 + billed retry rate). Orders = visits × paid conversion; starts = visits × studio start rate. Monthly AI = starts × AI/start; AI/order = monthly AI/orders. Digital contribution/order = price − original card fee − refund allowance − variable operations − AI/order. Monthly contribution = orders × (price − payment fee/order − refund allowance/order − variable operations/order) − monthly AI spend, even when orders are zero. Operating profit = monthly contribution − fixed budget − marketing − modeled founder pay, before income tax, depreciation and unpriced legal costs.

Break-even orders are rounded up from fixed + marketing (+ founder pay) divided by contribution/order. They assume proportional traffic/starts/usage as orders scale: Conservative/Base/Growth 130/93/322 bootstrap and 1,610/553/723 with founder pay. This is not fixed-traffic conversion break-even. In a fixed-traffic sensitivity, studio AI spend stays constant while paid orders change. Division by zero and non-positive contribution return no meaningful break-even; a blank runway with no burn means cash-positive steady state, never guaranteed infinite runway.

The one-factor table includes conversion 1% and 3%, half/double traffic, four images/start, eight chats/start, studio starts 20%, funding allowances 0%, 5.5% and 10%, 2,000/8,000 image-output tokens, true-edit input tokens and 10% future billed retries. Each changes only the named factor against the currently selected saved scenario. Its default Main results are Base sensitivities. Shared scalar inputs are read explicitly without the sensitivity/SKU dimension; costs are allocated at the order, not summed twice.

## Cash, reserve and hypothetical ramp

Launch reserve = $500 contingency + 7/30 of monthly variable costs. Seven-day payout timing is an estimate; the reserve is held aside from usable cash, not expensed again. Monthly burn = max(0, negative operating profit). Finite runway = max(0, opening cash − reserve)/burn. Any opening below reserve is **UNFUNDED LAUNCH**, including a projected profitable Growth case with zero cash. $5,000 is an alternative funding hypothesis, not committed financing.

Formula-derived launch reserves are **$581.3446667/$616.011/$856.9139583** for Conservative/Base/Growth. The reserve is unchanged by the fixed-budget split because it depends on variable costs. Six-month bootstrap funding is **$1,548.0491/$616.011/$856.9140**; with founder pay it is **$19,548.0491/$14,399.151/$856.9140**. Three-month prelaunch plus six-month postlaunch ramp funding is **$2,335.5413/$2,416.011/$8,056.9140** bootstrap and **$29,335.5413/$25,199.151/$17,056.9140** with founder pay. Conservative’s revised operating losses are **$161.1174/$3,161.1174** monthly and its break-even orders are **130/1,610**.

At hypothetical **$1,000** opening cash, Conservative runway is **2.60/0.13 months** bootstrap/founder; Base is cash-positive steady state bootstrap/0.17 months founder; Growth is cash-positive steady state in both, subject to reserve and ramp. At **$5,000**, Conservative is **27.43/1.40 months**, Base cash-positive/1.91, and Growth remains cash-positive in steady state. Explicit **$0** does not fund any launch reserve. Blank opening cash means **Opening cash unknown**; cash balances, funding gaps and runway are unavailable rather than zero.

**Runway months are measured from the hypothetical opening cash and ignore prelaunch burn.** Opening cash is dated **2026-10-01**, before the three prelaunch months. Base begins the hypothetical **2027-01-01** launch at **-$800** with $1,000 opening cash: $1,000 − 3 × $600. The January closing balance then includes January’s projected operating cash flow. Base’s bootstrap ramp gap is **$1,416.011**, so its status is **“Opening cash covers reserve only; ramp unfunded”**, not a funded launch. At zero conversion the gap is **$6,826.0377**. When opening cash is below reserve the status stays **UNFUNDED LAUNCH**; only zero bootstrap ramp gap permits **“Reserve funded; ramp funded.”** Founder-pay ramp funding is shown separately and must be checked separately.

The dated projection is an illustrative calendar, not history or a launch commitment: start October 1, 2026; hypothetical production launch January 1, 2027; three prelaunch months; projection ends December 2027. Prelaunch revenue and variable sales costs are zero; fixed and marketing burn continues. Base prelaunch burn is $600/month bootstrap or $3,600/month founder. Constant postlaunch run rates deliberately omit ramp growth and seasonality rather than invent evidence. Negative projected cash remains visible and signals financing is required; the model does not pretend an unfunded business can continue spending in reality.
