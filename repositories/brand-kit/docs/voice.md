# Voice

**Rule: clarity over cleverness. Lead with what the user gets, then tell them exactly what to do next.**

The product is playful; the words are not. Delight comes from the stub, the sound and the tear. Copy keeps the path to a finished stub obvious and fast.

## Principles

1. **Benefit first, action second.** "Get a stub you'll want to frame. Drop your ticket." Say what they get before what they do.
2. **One action per screen.** Each screen has one primary verb. Secondary actions are quieter and fewer.
3. **Buttons are verbs.** Use "Print it", "Save to your wall", "Tear to finish". Never "Submit", "OK" or "Continue".
4. **Specific beats vague.** Numbers, names and prices: "Framed print, $89", not "Premium options".
5. **Short.** Titles and buttons are at most 8 words, and body text at most 2 sentences.
6. **Plain words.** Say "screenshot", "ticket", "stub" and "wall", not "OCR", "template", "asset" or "collection item".
7. **No puns, no exclamation marks, no "Oops".** Errors say what happened and how to fix it.
8. **Sentence case** everywhere except stub artwork (each edition sets its own case).

Stub content (facts printed on the stub) is different from UI copy. "Night 2 of 3 · 7°C · Rain" is data and stays terse.

|---|---|---|
